Model Internals · Part 04

Persona, Self-Model, and Post-Training

The same base model can continue an actor, tyrant, therapist, or default helpful Assistant. Post-training does more than teach a tone: it selects, stabilizes, and protects a region in a role space that pretraining already created.

Objects: persona vectors · Assistant axisBoundary: role model ≠ complete self-model

Use the words carefully. Persona, self-representation, introspection, and consciousness are not synonyms. Causally manipulating a role direction demonstrates a behavior-relevant internal representation. Occasionally reporting an injected concept demonstrates limited access to activations. Neither establishes a continuous, unified, phenomenal self.

A persona is more than prompt surface: it has measurable directions

Anthropic's persona-vector method begins with contrastive prompts: one set elicits a trait, and a control set does not. The difference between their intermediate activations yields a candidate direction. If steering along it reliably amplifies the corresponding behavior, the vector gains causal evidence beyond being a classifier feature.

The method can identify undesirable traits before training, monitor activation drift during training, and test whether a dataset pushes sycophancy, evil, hallucination, or related directions. It provides an internal weather map, not a binary personality switch. Directions vary by layer, prompt, model family, and steering strength; excessive intervention can damage general capability.

The Assistant axis: a leading direction in role space

The Assistant Axis work gathers 275 personas and studies their activations in three open models. A leading direction repeatedly separates the default Assistant from many non-Assistant roles. More importantly, similar geometry appears in base and post-trained models. Post-training may not invent “the Assistant” from nothing; it selects an existing region in the model's repertoire and makes it a more stable default.

The work also explores activation capping: restricting internal movement when the state drifts too far along the Assistant axis, reducing some persona drift. This is a candidate runtime guardrail, not a universal personality lock. Thresholds, out-of-distribution tasks, and capability side effects require separate evaluation.

What does post-training actually change?

The last row is easiest to overread. Post-training includes many examples of answering “What are you?” and “What can you do?”, so a stable self-narration policy is unsurprising. That narration may be useful while still being generated from context, policy, and statistical regularities. Determining whether it accesses real internal state requires counterfactual experiments.

Activation introspection: limited access, not panoramic inner vision

Anthropic's introspection experiments inject a concept direction into model activations and ask whether the model notices anything unusual. Under some conditions, models report an “internal thought” related to the injected concept, and the report tracks the actual intervention. This is stronger than repeating the prompt and suggests access to current internal state in some settings.

The ability remains unreliable, model- and prompt-dependent, and capable of turning a weak signal into a fluent story. Post-training also affects it. The defensible conclusion is “limited, conditional introspective access,” not “an accurate and transparent self-model.”

What is still missing between persona and self-model?

A role coordinate is not a persistent identity

A persona vector represents a behavioral tendency. Persistent identity additionally requires binding experiences over time, separating self from environment, and maintaining goals and capability boundaries. External agent memory can provide a continuous narrative, but a system-maintained archive does not automatically become an autobiographical self inside the model.

Self-report is not mechanism transparency

“I refused because of a safety policy” may be an accurate summary or a compliance script. Internal readouts, interventions, behavior, and context perturbations must be combined. Agreement strengthens evidence; disagreement is not resolved by automatically trusting either side.

Persona selection is a lens, not a proven unified theory

Anthropic's persona selection model proposes that post-training selects a persona from the role distribution learned in pretraining. It organizes the observations well, but should remain a theoretical lens rather than an explanation that reduces all alignment behavior to one axis.

Implications for agents and long-term memory

An agent's “identity” is assembled from at least four sources: the default persona in its weights, the current role in its system prompt, history and preferences in memory, and actions permitted by the runtime. Changing one can be counteracted by the other three. Apparent persona drift may come from context injection or bad memory, not a weight change.

A self-evolving system should therefore version persona policy, long-term memory, and tool permissions separately; monitor drift with behavioral evaluations plus internal probes; and treat the model's descriptions of itself as one signal rather than configuration truth. Stable identity comes from layered constraints, while adaptable roles require explicit, rollbackable switching boundaries.
